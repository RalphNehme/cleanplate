Menus that list only a few meals (say, a chain page showing just its promo items) are pulled toward the average restaurant, as if {{ prior_meals }} typical meals were added, so a short page can't swing to an extreme. Averaging many meals also bunches most restaurants between about 30 and 70, so the average is stretched: shown score = 50 + (average − {{ center }}) × {{ stretch | round(1) }}, capped at 100. This keeps the order exactly the same and only makes differences easier to see. The weights follow mainstream dietary guidance (more vegetables, whole grains, legumes and unsaturated fats; less sodium, added sugar, saturated fat and processed meat), but the exact numbers are CleanPlate's own design and aren't clinically validated.

Unknowns don't count against a restaurant. If a menu only lists dish names, or doesn't print sodium, those parts get no points either way. Instead the page shows a lower confidence. Marketing claims like "natural" or "premium" don't earn points.

Restaurants without a readable menu aren't scored or shown, so no score is a guess from the restaurant's name or cuisine.

Seed oils

Many people want to avoid seed oils (canola, soybean, corn, sunflower, "vegetable" oil), so CleanPlate records any cooking oil a menu names, and visitors can report what a kitchen uses. You can filter a city's map to "No seed oils" to see restaurants whose menu or visitors name olive oil, avocado oil, butter, ghee or tallow.

The oil doesn't change the food score. Mainstream nutrition guidance, including the American Heart Association's, recommends unsaturated oils such as olive, canola and soybean over saturated fats such as butter and tallow, and the evidence doesn't support a blanket seed-oil penalty. Butter, ghee and tallow count toward saturated fat like any other ingredient.

Hygiene score

Shown smaller, from each restaurant's latest health inspection.

New York City

Violation points become 1–100 in line with the official grade (A is 85–100, B is 70–84, C is below 70). A closure by the Health Department caps it at 30.

Chicago

Violations are weighted by severity (Priority 7, Priority Foundation 4, Core 2). A Fail caps it at 60 and a Pass with Conditions at 84. Inspections from July 2018 onward.
